## Mechanics and vocabulary for a new agent

Read the repository's root `SIMULATION.md` before implementing an action; it describes the actual Rust model, which is the authority for this research. The simulator advances one **redstone tick** per call, or one tenth of a second. A game tick is half a redstone tick. Speed in blocks per second is `10 × net forward blocks / simulator ticks`. Two blocks in eight ticks are 2.5 bps; three in ten are 3 bps; approximately 3,333 in 10,000 ticks are 3.333 bps. A **segment/carrier** is a group that travels together in a particular action, not a special block type. `m` means it advances one X cell during an action slot, `w` that it waits. A cycle should restore the relative physical arrangement translated in +X. A push or pull can move another piston's base, allowing more net travel than a single piston stroke.

Coordinate convention is forward `+X`, vertical `Y`, lateral `Z`. Direction integers in the Python format are `+X=0`, `-X=1`, `+Y=2`, `-Y=3`, `+Z=4`, `-Z=5`. All directions on observers, rods, and pistons must be transformed when rotating or mirroring a layout. Chunk boundaries lie every 16 cells in X/Z. The saved world phase modulo 16 affects chunk processing order, so a machine tested only at phase 0 is not robustly established.

Every tick has a power stage and then a piston stage. Stationary redstone blocks and rods soft-power adjacent pistons. A powered observer soft-powers only an adjacent piston it faces. Stationary rods and powered observers also **hard-power** a solid block directly ahead; that powered solid can in turn power adjacent pistons. In this simulator the solid types eligible for hard power are slime, honey, smooth stone, and glazed terracotta. Glass, pistons, rods, redstone, and observers are not solid for that rule. The piston ignores a power source or hard-powered neighbour directly in front of its face. Moving sources emit no power, moving targets receive none, and all observers turn off at the end of a power stage. A moved observer becomes powered only when its movement finishes; the pulse is consumed in the next power stage. Its serialized direction points to its **output**, not to its detection face. Ordinary block-change observer detection and detector rails are not simulated.

A piston has four states: `0` retracted, `1` extending, `2` extended, `3` retracting. A powered state-0 piston starts extension and creates moving destinations; state 1 finishes the movement and enters state 2. An unpowered state-2 piston starts retracting; state 3 finishes and returns to 0. Thus a clean extend/retract sequence occupies four simulator ticks. A normal piston does not pull. A sticky piston can pull during retraction; discovery starts **two cells ahead of its base**, because its arm occupies the intervening cell. The arm cell is removed before discovery. The initiating piston and its arm do not count toward its own movement limit. A failed sticky pull still retracts without the load. An unpowered, nonmoving piston has its `angry` flag cleared before update; do not use initial `angry=True` as a substitute for power. Format-valid extended states are allowed as an initialization experiment, but must converge to a sustained translated cycle.

For each action the simulator discovers a source set before moving anything. Begin with the block directly ahead for an extension, or two cells ahead for a sticky pull. Every occupied destination adds a pushed obstruction. Every selected slime or honey cell adds its six face-neighbours when adhesion permits; repeat both rules to closure. Slime and honey do not adhere to each other or glazed terracotta. They adhere to compatible ordinary blocks and movable pistons. Glazed terracotta can still join by direct obstruction even though slime/honey cannot grab it by adhesion. An immovable neighbour reached only through adhesion is skipped, but an immovable destination fails the action. Moving blocks, piston arms, and pistons in states 1–3 are immovable. The initiating piston is specially nonsticky and immovable for its own search. The source set's distinct cells count against `push_limit`; two simultaneous pistons do not combine capacity for one connected set. A `push limit exceeded` trace may stop discovery early, so use a higher-limit diagnostic copy to see the intended complete set, and label it diagnostic.

Moving ownership matters. A successful action removes all sources before filling destinations, marks destinations moving, and records their **destination** positions on the initiating piston. They remain moving and cannot supply power or move again until that piston finishes. A piston carried across a chunk boundary can encounter a second update opportunity within one tick depending on chunk order. Rust shuffles a fixed list of occupied chunks, then shuffles piston candidates within each chunk using the saved RNG. It does not promise one universal piston order. Same-tick cross-chunk or angry-piston tricks need explicit RNG and X/Z-phase testing; a fast single-seed run is only a lead.

## The existing pure-push ring model

The `astra_ringgen_safe.py` family generates a loop of alternating slime/honey segments using a **two-action-slot lag**. A carrier moves for `N` slots and waits two; its source/rear carrier follows two slots later. A firing piston stays fixed during its extension and retraction, then the other carrier picks it up. The loop closes only when `2*k` is divisible by `N+2`, where `k` is the segment count. This gives `N=2,k=2` for 2.5 bps, `N=3,k=5` for 3 bps, and `N=4,k=3` (or 6) for 3.333 bps. The formula is a property of this template, **not** a universal theorem about other schedules such as the new `mwmw` lead. Adding one push to a two-segment engine does not automatically create a 3 bps engine.

For the pure template, interface `i` has phase `p_i = (2*i) mod (N+2)`. With axial offset `h` and local phase `p`, its front sticky cross is at `x=h+1+min(p,N)`, shared redstone at `x=h+min(p,N)`, and rear pickup cross at `x=h-1+max(0,p-2)`. A piston `j` that has not yet fired is at `x=h+p`; one that has fired is at `x=h+max(j,p-2)`, with state 2 and an arm at `x+1` exactly when `p=j+1`. This allowed initialized phase states rather than demanding all pistons start retracted. N4 uses four piston sites around a shared redstone center and a five-cell front sticky cross; N3 uses three of the same four arms. Rear pickup covers every site because the actual shared-power firing permutation can vary. The generator's `make(N, centers, seed, limit)` returns a Python flyer, sticky counts and geometry detail. Its seed randomizes geometry; `Flyer(rng_state=5, ...)` in the generator fixes **simulation** RNG separately. Keep those two seeds distinct in experiment records.

The safe generator avoids many dangerous piston/power adjacencies but is conservative. It routes sticky connectors in a fixed bounding box with a path-cost cap and sequential random choices; failing to route says little about an unseen topology. It can also yield a format-valid candidate that stalls in Rust. The smart generator tests unions of possible piston positions for different orders, which can reject valid timed contacts. The side-contact generator reduces some pickup cells but has disconnected components and segment-merging examples. Before expanding those families, check mandatory-cell collisions **before** dictionary insertion, connectivity of all required pickup components, phase-by-phase unintended adhesion, piston power truth tables, and actual Rust movement sets. A geometry's static sticky-cell count is only a lower bound on its action load; power blocks, pistons and destination obstructions often determine the failure.
