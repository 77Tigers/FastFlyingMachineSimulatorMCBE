**Piston timing guide (derive designs from this, don't ask the user):** actions happen at even ticks; a "slot" is 2 ticks. A piston powered at slot k extends at k (pushes its front block +1), retracts at k+1 if unpowered then (sticky: retraction pulls the block two cells ahead of the base), and is movable again from k+2. So a **rigid pusher firing at k needs its carrier still at k, k+1**; a **rigid sticky pulling at k extends at k-1, needs its carrier still at k-1, k**. A sticky is powered when it *extends* (same slot as its power source arrives) and pulls when power goes away. A sticky pulls the segment **behind** it. Redstone blocks give power purely by adjacency, so a redstone block on segment X powers a piston on another segment only at the relative offset where they touch: with mmww/wwmm segments, the offset at the fire slot is unique if the block touches the piston from the side, so it switches off by itself after X moves. **Power trick (user):** the redstone block on X powers the pusher that pushes X *and* the sticky (on the segment in front) that pulls X: both switch on at the push slot, and X's own move switches the sticky off, so it pulls X one slot later.

**Observerless timing (2026-10-11, [findings](WIP/experiments/observerless_20261011/FINDINGS.md)):** steady sources (rods, redstone blocks, two bodies lining up) cannot pulse. A piston retracts only if something moved after it extended, so count the moves. *Self-resetting push:* power a pusher from the body it pushes. *Shuttle:* a sticky pusher with a rod in front of its target pushes the rod along and pulls it back alone two ticks later, which is a free extra event. Sense the rod against the body that did *not* move. Example: an observerless 1.666 bps flyer, 11 blocks, PL6, 80/80, unbanked.

**Rigid-segment design rules (2026-10-03, see `WIP/experiments/rigid_chain_20261003/FINDINGS.md`):**

- Piston update order inside a tick is random, so avoid every *race*: (a) a segment must never move into a cell of another segment that moves in the same slot (e.g. a victim left right behind its puller's sticky when both move next slot); (b) a hand-off ("rider") piston may only be adjacent to a moving carrier's glue when it should ride it â€” at its own firing slot it is still state 0 and gets dragged, unless that move is the push it initiates itself; a rider extended at slot start (state 2) is immovable and safe.
- **Leaf races are SAFE (verified 2026-10-04, `rigid_chain_20261003/leaf_race_test.py`):** if glue of segment J touches a NON-glue block (idle piston, redstone block, observer) of segment O and BOTH move +X in the same tick by their own pistons, either order gives the same result: J first drags the leaf +1 (where it was going anyway), O first makes it a moving block, which is immovable and skipped by adhesion. Conditions: leaf not a firing piston, leaf's destination empty, and the leaf counts toward the push count of whichever goes first (so add it to the load). This is a cheap instance of "make both update orders give an acceptable outcome". Untested: a dragged redstone block whose power is needed later in the same tick. Rule (a)/(b) above still hold for glue-glue and for segments that do NOT move in that tick.
- 5-block middle segment exists (alternating templates, redstone block from the segment in front lands on the free common neighbour of the diagonal sticky/pusher pair). Load bound for any 2.5 bps flyer: PL >= 2N/P.
- Closure stretch identity: a push-push pair A (mmww) <-> B (wwmm) needs (A contact x - A closure pusher x) + (B contact x - B closure pusher x) = 4; same 4 for a pull-pull front pair. Chain links have zero stretch. More generally the push/pull graph has an x-potential (pushed and puller segments sit ahead), so the rearmost segment's pusher and the frontmost segment's puller must reach across: stretch cannot be avoided, only placed. A closed ring of n alternating chain links (K pushes K+1, K+1 pulls K) needs BOTH sum(contact x - pusher x) = 2n AND sum(sticky x - pulled-glue x) = 2n (links advance +3/+1), i.e. each segment's sticky sits ~4 ahead of its pusher: a pure ring costs ~8 blocks per segment. Exception: "double pull" start caps (A pulled at s0 by a wmmw segment ahead and at s1 by the chain, merged move at s1) and double pushes have no ahead-cycle, so no stretch.
- With only mmww/wwmm every segment gets exactly one push (rear) and one pull (front), so a flyer must close; closures need x-stretch (~2 per closing segment); caps need a third word type or hand-off pistons.
- **Leaf PUSH races are also SAFE (verified 2026-10-04, `rigid_sat_20261004/leafpush_test.py`):** a mover may move a block INTO a non-glue block (piston incl. -X sticky, redstone, observer) of a segment that moves in the same tick by its own cause: first mover pushes the leaf +1 as an obstruction, else the cell is freed. Leaf's destination must be empty; the leaf counts toward the first mover's load. Pushing into co-moving *glue* is still a forbidden race.
- **Merged moves (2026-10-04):** two co-moving segments may move as ONE deterministic action when the cause acts on one and it carries the other by obstruction (a block right behind the other's glue) or same-material glue; load = sum. Needed whenever a carrier's piston sits right behind another segment's glue in a slot where both move. Timing law: a "double push" end (F pushed at k by X, at k+1 by M whose word is still at k+1,k+2) forces M to co-move with F at k (merge M carries F) AND forces whatever pushes M at k to co-move with M again one slot earlier (second merge). A "double pull" start (A pulled at 0 by a wmmw segment N ahead and at 1 by the chain) needs one merge (A's idle pusher carries N at slot 1) and NO closure stretch, because every push/pull agrees on who is ahead.
- **Power for third-word pistons:** a wmmw/mwwm segment's pistons have no redstone source with a unique relative offset; use an observer on a segment whose two pulses fall at different relative offsets (e.g. on K1 for N's sticky at slot 3) or a rod-AND.
- **Exact solver (use it before writing another greedy search):** `WIP/experiments/rigid_sat_20261004/satflyer.py` (ortools CP-SAT; `pip install ortools`) encodes all rules above plus riders (hand-off pistons) and merged moves; validated against rigid.check (70/70 mutations), the banked PL8 (accepted at 8, rejected at 7) and real-sim runs. ## Mechanics and proof contract

One Rust tick is 0.1 seconds. Speed is `10 Ã— displacement / ticks`. Score is final minus initial minimum X **within the same in-memory simulation**, exactly 10,000 ticks. Saving renormalizes coordinates; never score by comparing separately saved minima. Directions: `+X=0,-X=1,+Y=2,-Y=3,+Z=4,-Z=5`.

Power runs before piston updates. Redstone/rods soft-power adjacent pistons; observers output only along their direction. Rods/observers hard-power a solid ahead: slime, honey, stone, glazed terracotta. Pistons ignore sources directly in front. Moving sources/targets do not supply/receive power. A moved observer pulses when movement finishes; the next power stage consumes it. Initial unpowered `angry=True` does not start a stationary piston.

States 0/1/2/3 are retracted/extending/extended/retracting; a clean cycle takes four ticks. Sticky retraction removes its front/arm cell, then discovers from **two cells ahead**, moving toward the base. Moving cells, arms and state-1/2/3 pistons are immovable. Adhesion skips an immovable neighbor; an immovable destination fails. Slime/honey do not adhere to each other or glazed terracotta. Discovery recursively adds allowed sticky face-neighbors and occupied destinations. The initiating piston/arm do not count. Two pistons cannot pool capacity.

**User rule (2026-10-01): a screened candidate that travels more than about 30 blocks, even if it stalls or fails, is likely close to a good design â€” investigate it (trace the first failure, try local repairs) instead of discarding it.** Screen 120â€“300 ticks, then longer, then exactly 10,000 at the encoded claimed limit. Verify permanent-kind conservation every tick and full translated repeat including states, moving bits, observer power and owner lists. Run all 80 phase/RNG samples before banking; this remains a sample, not a universal RNG proof. No ballast/debris or friendly-start tricks. Preserve references and unrelated dirty files. Simulator results require separate Bedrock testing before claiming in-game compatibility.

## Suggested don'ts (unvetted)

An agent (J2, 2026-10-02) suggested the following advice, distilled from failed attempts. Treat it as heuristics, not rules:

1. **Check what each cell carries before trimming a body.** A cell may exist only to carry another body's piston (tm_smol B15's tail carries B9's pusher B0); deleting it strands the piston. Move the role, not the cell.

2. **Check who rides the back before redesigning the front.** Read the rider ledger first: in 3bps_original no front piston rides a rear body, so front redesigns cannot lower rear loads.

3. **Constrain and validate role plans.** Limit carriers to nearby bodies and pullers to bodies in front of their victim, and require the model to reproduce the real flyer's loads before trusting its predictions (`tm_smol_rebalance_20261001/j2_front_smol/layered.py`).

4. **Check the generator can express the plan.** If the plan needs a piston to change carrier mid-cycle and the generator only has rigid carriers, it returns "0 ports" every time. Check capability before running it.

5. **Keep a victim observer clear of the puller's new glue,** and make sure its other pulses power no piston. Otherwise the puller drags it and it fires a slot early (the B45->B37 near-miss).

6. **Count a pull's cost on the victim, not just the added sticky.** A victim 3 cells behind the sticky needs about 3 observer support cells, about +3 load (tm_smol B24 always went to 14).
