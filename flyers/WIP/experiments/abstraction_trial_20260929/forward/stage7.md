An eight-tick Rust screen of `speculative_assembly.flyer` gave action counts: tick 0 one retraction/5 moved and one empty extension; tick 2 one retraction/6 moved; ticks 4 and 6 no action. The first concrete discrepancy is t0 transport: stage 2 requires A1 and B1 to ride A, but the five moved sources are only the A rail, and inspection after one tick leaves the pistons at their initial `(3,0,4)` and `(5,0,6)`. The script placed them at later coordinates without reconstructing their cycle-boundary support. At t2 the B rail drags A1 from X=3 to X=4; after four ticks A1 is at `(4,0,4)`, while stage 3 requires its t4 pull base at X=3. Missing later pulses are a separate unimplemented requirement, not a simulator contradiction.

No long run or research-log audit was appropriate because the short screen failed. There is no claim of recurrence, PL10 performance, or robustness to alternate piston order. The primitive action probes do not complete a stage-5 cycle interface: timed pulse generation and absence, repeated pickup and release, and full-phase keepouts remain unverified. The assembly failure arises first from my initialization/transport mistake, then from the naive cross-body route and missing power pulses. It does not distinguish whether a better bounded motif placement could compose the full abstract design.
