## The user's rear-push-to-front-pull idea

There is a promising **schedule window**, even though no pull flyer has been built yet. In the current N4 three-segment loop, initial phases are `[0,2,4]`; over six two-tick action slots, segment0 moves in slots0–3, segment1 in slots4–5 and0–1, and segment2 in slots2–5. At slot3, segment0 needs its fourth forward move while segment1 waits. Thus segment1 could, in principle, pull segment0 forward and replace the last push from segment2, transferring a demanding load away from the source at the back.

The current PL22 geometry does not offer a cheap direct pull. Immediately before slot3, segment0 and segment1 have an axial gap of four cells at their matching transverse sites. A sticky piston facing -X needs its base two cells ahead of the target; mounting that piston to segment1 would require at least one extra axial connector, plus a correctly timed pulse and pickup space. A piston placed using the **post-move** segment0 position is one cell too far forward and pulls air. The next concrete experiment is to vary the axial span vector while preserving loop closure, seeking a pre-move gap of three with safe contact. Then add a backward sticky piston to the stationary front segment, pulse it in slot2 and retract it in slot3, while disabling one back-source push. Verify power, retraction target, all movement sets, and sustained period with Rust. No speed or push-limit claim follows from timing alone.
